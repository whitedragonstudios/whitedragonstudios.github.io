Getting to Paradise

Getting to and around the island

Almost every visitor reaches Taniti by air. The island's small airport welcomes small jets and propeller planes, and an expansion is under way so that larger jets can land within the next few years. Until then, most itineraries include one connection through a regional hub, followed by a short, scenic hop over open water.

The airport itself is compact and friendly. You will step off the plane into warm, salty air, walk a few minutes to the terminal and collect your bags in less time than it takes to find a seat at a big-city gate. Taxis, shuttles and the local car rental desk are all within sight of the exit, so you can be on your way to your lodging within the hour.

A small number of travelers arrive by sea. A small cruise ship docks in Yellow Leaf Bay for one night each week, which gives its passengers an evening to wander Taniti City, enjoy a local dinner and watch the sun set behind the volcano. Many of those passengers like the island so much that they plan a longer return trip by air.

Once you arrive, getting around is easy. Taniti City is fairly flat and very walkable, and the area around Merriton Landing is simple to explore on foot. Public buses run through Taniti City every day, private buses serve the rest of the island, taxis are available in the city, and bikes and helmets are available from several vendors. Arriving by Air

Airline information

Taniti's small airport serves small jets and propeller planes, so most routes connect through a regional hub. Flights are short and the views of turquoise water and green peaks are worth a window seat.

Seats on the smaller aircraft fill quickly during the busy season, so book early and pack light. Weight limits on propeller planes can be stricter than on larger jets, so check your airline's baggage rules before you head to the airport.

Arrival day is easy to plan around. Most flights land in the morning or early afternoon, which leaves time to check in, change into beachwear and still catch the sunset over Yellow Leaf Bay. If your flight lands late, let your lodging know so someone can wait up for you.

Good to know

  • Book flights at least two months ahead for holidays and the dry season.
  • Pack a small carry-on, because propeller planes have tighter bag limits.
  • Keep a copy of your lodging address handy for the airport shuttle desk.

Arriving by Ship

Cruise information

A small cruise ship docks in Yellow Leaf Bay for one night each week. Passengers can stroll straight from the pier into Taniti City, and the beaches are only a short walk away.

With just one night in port, planning matters. Many cruise guests book a morning volcano tour or a lagoon snorkeling trip ahead of time, then spend the evening at a restaurant in town. Shops and restaurants may close on national holidays, so check the calendar before you sail.

If you are a cruise guest, a good rule is to pick one big activity and one relaxing one. A morning volcano tour followed by an afternoon on the beach lets you see the highlights without rushing, and you will still have time to find a table at one of Taniti City's restaurants before the ship sails.

Good to know

  • Be back at the pier at least 30 minutes before the ship departs.
  • Bring a lightweight rain jacket, because showers pass quickly.
  • Carry some U.S. dollars for small shops and market stalls.

Public Transport

Public transport information

Public buses serve Taniti City every day from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. Private buses cover the rest of the island, and taxis are available in Taniti City.

Taniti City is fairly flat and very walkable, so many visitors only need transport for longer trips, such as heading from the airport to their lodging or out to the rainforest. Keep small bills handy for bus fares and tips.

Getting around without a car is part of the charm. Bus drivers are happy to point out the right stop, taxi drivers double as informal tour guides, and the walk from the bay to the market is flat and shady. Just remember that service winds down late in the evening, so plan your ride home in advance.

Good to know

  • City buses run from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. every day.
  • Ask your lodging to call a taxi if you are out after dark.
  • Wear comfortable shoes, since Taniti City is built for walking.

Renting a car

Car rental information

Rental cars are available from a local agency near the airport. Roads around Taniti City are easy to drive, while the winding routes into the interior reward patient drivers with sweeping views.

Bikes and helmets can also be rented from several vendors, and helmets are required by law. If you are staying near Merriton Landing, you may find that you do not need a car at all.

Driving gives you the freedom to chase waterfalls, pull over at roadside fruit stands and watch the sunrise from the north shore. Roads in the interior are narrow and winding, so take your time, keep headlights on in the rain and watch for cyclists, who are required by law to wear helmets.

Good to know

  • Reserve early for holiday weeks, when cars sell out.
  • Fill the tank before heading to the interior.
  • Helmets are required by law for anyone renting a bike.
